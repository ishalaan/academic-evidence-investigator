Legal, Social, Ethical and Professional ConsiderationsLegal:- External APIs must be used in accordance with their terms, licensing conditions and rate limits.- API credentials are stored in environment variables and are excluded from source control.- The system retrieves and displays academic metadata and links rather than redistributing full copyrighted publications.Social:- Access to LLM-powered research tools may vary between institutions, students and regions.- Differences in digital literacy and access to paid or rate-limited services may affect who benefits from the system.- The tool is intended to assist researchers rather than replace academic judgement.Ethical:- LLMs may hallucinate, introduce bias or misrepresent evidence.- The system reduces this risk by grounding reports in retrieved sources, validating structured outputs and preserving bibliographic metadata deterministically.- The Reporter is prevented from inventing citation metadata, and limitations are shown explicitly to the user.- Human review remains necessary before using generated findings in academic work.Professional:- The implementation emphasises reproducibility, traceability, testing and secure credential handling.- External-service failures are handled transparently rather than hidden.- Deterministic validation, deduplication and ranking make key evidence-processing decisions easier to inspect and test.- The system communicates its limitations and should not be treated as an authoritative or exhaustive literature-review tool.
