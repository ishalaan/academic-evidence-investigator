Research Report

Research Question

How does generative AI affect student learning outcomes in higher education?

Summary

Generative AI (GenAI) shows mixed but notable impacts on student learning outcomes in higher education, with evidence highlighting both opportunities and challenges. Studies indicate that GenAI tools can enhance academic support through functions like grammar correction, research assistance, and instant feedback, which improve task efficiency and conceptual understanding (Alkhatib et al., 2026). For example, one study found that GenAI aids in organising ideas and strengthening problem-solving skills, though its effectiveness depends on students’ ability to engage critically with the technology (Alkhatib et al., 2026). Similarly, a systematic review noted that GenAI-based assessment systems offer adaptive, real-time feedback, potentially increasing engagement and personalising learning experiences (D, 2026). However, these benefits are contingent on factors like student engagement and AI literacy, which mediate the relationship between GenAI use and academic performance (Alkhatib et al., 2026). A framework for integrating GenAI emphasises aligning its use with pedagogical goals and institutional readiness, suggesting that strategic implementation can enhance learning outcomes while addressing gaps in technological preparedness (Riaz and Mushtaq, 2024).

The evidence also reveals variability in GenAI’s impact across disciplines and contexts. While some studies report improved engagement and performance, others highlight risks such as over-reliance on AI, which may undermine critical thinking or academic integrity (Hon, 2025). For instance, a Nigerian study found that GenAI combined with learning analytics could personalise learning but raised concerns about ethical use and academic dishonesty (Elenode, 2026). Similarly, a case study in Vietnamese higher education noted that GenAI tools like ChatGPT support language learning through grammar correction and vocabulary practice, yet challenges include skill imbalance and ethical dilemmas ("EXPLORING THE IMPACT OF GENERATIVE AI ON ENGLISH LANGUAGE LEARNING IN VIETNAMESE HIGHER EDUCATION: A CASE STUDY OF TWO VIETNAMESE UNIVERSITIES", 2025). These findings underscore the need for balanced integration, where GenAI complements rather than replaces traditional learning methods. The role of AI literacy becomes critical here, as students with higher AI literacy are better equipped to leverage GenAI for deeper learning (Alkhatib et al., 2026).

Practical implications suggest that institutions must prioritize training and policy frameworks to maximise GenAI’s benefits. A study on AI art applications in higher education found that generative tools support creative and interdisciplinary projects, but structural barriers like access disparities and pedagogical misalignment hinder their adoption (Rong et al., 2025). This aligns with broader calls for institutional support, including faculty training and ethical guidelines, to ensure GenAI is used responsibly (McKenzie and Day, 2025). Additionally, the effectiveness of GenAI varies by discipline; for example, its utility in language learning is well-documented, but its impact on technical or theoretical subjects remains less clear ("EXPLORING THE IMPACT OF GENERATIVE AI ON ENGLISH LANGUAGE LEARNING IN VIETNAMESE HIGHER EDUCATION: A CASE STUDY OF TWO VIETNAMESE UNIVERSITIES", 2025). These nuances highlight the importance of context-specific strategies, where GenAI is tailored to align with curricular goals and student needs.

Despite these insights, the evidence remains fragmented, with limited long-term data on GenAI’s sustained impact. Most studies focus on short-term engagement or specific applications, leaving gaps in understanding its broader educational outcomes (Hon, 2025). Furthermore, the reliance on self-reported data or small-scale implementations may limit generalisability (Elenode, 2026). Nevertheless, the consensus across studies is that GenAI has the potential to transform higher education when integrated thoughtfully. By addressing challenges like ethical use, faculty training, and equitable access, institutions can harness GenAI to enhance learning outcomes while fostering responsible technological literacy (Alkhatib et al., 2026).

Findings

  1. Generative AI-based assessment systems provide adaptive, real-time feedback, which may enhance personalisation and engagement compared to traditional methods (D, 2026).
  2. A quantitative study found that student engagement and AI literacy mediate the relationship between GenAI use and academic performance, with higher AI literacy correlating with better learning outcomes (Alkhatib et al., 2026).
  3. A Nigerian study combining GenAI with learning analytics reported improved personalised learning but raised concerns about ethical use and academic integrity risks (Elenode, 2026).
  4. In Vietnamese higher education, GenAI tools like ChatGPT support language learning through grammar correction and vocabulary practice, though challenges include skill imbalance and ethical dilemmas ("EXPLORING THE IMPACT OF GENERATIVE AI ON ENGLISH LANGUAGE LEARNING IN VIETNAMESE HIGHER EDUCATION: A CASE STUDY OF TWO VIETNAMESE UNIVERSITIES", 2025).
  5. A systematic review highlighted conflicting evidence, with some studies reporting increased engagement and others noting over-reliance on AI undermining critical thinking (Hon, 2025).

Limitations

  • The evidence highlights mixed findings on GenAI's impact, with some studies reporting enhanced engagement while others note risks like over-reliance or academic integrity issues (Hon, 2025). Further research comparing longitudinal outcomes across diverse institutional contexts would clarify consistent effects.
  • Many studies rely on self-reported data or qualitative insights (Riaz and Mushtaq, 2024), limiting objective measurement of learning gains. Empirical studies with standardised metrics could strengthen causal inferences about GenAI's role in academic performance.
  • Variability in institutional technological preparedness and pedagogical integration strategies (Elenode, 2026) suggests contextual factors may mediate GenAI's effectiveness. Cross-cultural comparative studies could identify scalable implementation frameworks.
  • Ethical considerations, such as AI literacy gaps and bias risks (Shuvo et al., 2025), are underexplored. Research examining long-term impacts on critical thinking and digital citizenship would address these gaps.
  • Most studies focus on tool usage rather than pedagogical design (Alkhatib et al., 2026). Investigating how GenAI integration aligns with curricular goals could reveal more actionable insights for educational outcomes.

A quantitative research design was adopted, and data were collected through a structured questionnaire from 350 undergraduate and postgraduate students enrolled in higher education institutions. The results revealed that GenAI usage had a significant positive effect on student learning outcomes (β = 0.41, p < 0.001). Overall, the findings suggest that GenAI alone is not sufficient to ensure improved learning outcomes; its effectiveness depends largely on student engagement and AI literacy competencies. The study provides practical implications for universities, teachers, and policymakers to develop AI literacy programs and student-centered GenAI learning strategies in higher education.
