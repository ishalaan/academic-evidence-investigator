Research Report

Research Question

What are the main applications of large language models in education?

Summary

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly applied in education to enhance personalization, assessment, and accessibility. A key application is adaptive learning systems, which use LLMs to tailor educational content to individual needs. For instance, S3 highlights how LLMs enable dynamic learning paths by analyzing student performance and adjusting instructional sequences in real time, while S4 emphasizes their role in generating context-aware feedback and assessments. Similarly, S5 proposes an audience-based framework that integrates LLMs to address learners' cognitive, affective, and social needs through enhanced semantic understanding. These systems reduce the limitations of one-size-fits-all approaches, offering scalable solutions for diverse educational contexts (Boumezough, Ouhmed and Lahcen, no date; Iqbal and Kene, 2026; Lu et al., 2026).

Another critical application is automated assessment and course evaluation. S2 introduces E-EVAL, a benchmark designed to evaluate LLMs' performance in Chinese K-12 subjects, demonstrating their potential to standardize grading across disciplines. Meanwhile, S9 explores LLMs in higher education, showing they can analyze course data to provide quantitative insights for curricular improvements. However, effectiveness depends on fine-tuning and prompt engineering, as noted in S9. These tools not only streamline evaluation processes but also address biases and inefficiencies in traditional methods (Hou et al., 2024; Yuan and Hu, no date).

LLMs also support language education through personalized practice and immersive experiences. S1 discusses their use in foreign language learning, enabling interactive listening, speaking, and writing exercises that adapt to learners' proficiency levels. S7 further illustrates this by examining rural Indian classrooms, where LLMs offer round-the-clock academic support and reduce teacher workload. Despite infrastructural challenges, volunteers report that LLMs can bridge resource gaps, though adoption is hindered by digital literacy and language barriers (Yuxuan et al., 2025; goyal et al., 2025).

Integration with multimodal technologies expands LLMs' educational utility. S6 describes a system combining LLMs and computer vision to create personalized learning assistants, using visual and textual data to adapt content dynamically. S10 complements this by proposing explainable AI tutors that provide human-readable justifications for instructional decisions, enhancing trust and engagement. These innovations highlight LLMs' potential to create interactive, responsive learning environments, though ethical concerns around data privacy and algorithmic bias remain unresolved (Hosam, 2026; T and Rajakumari, 2025).

While LLMs offer transformative possibilities, challenges such as technical limitations, ethical considerations, and equitable access persist. S8 underscores the need for robust infrastructure and policies to address disparities in AI adoption, particularly in under-resourced regions. As demonstrated by S7 and S8, successful implementation requires balancing technological potential with pedagogical and socio-economic realities. Future developments must prioritize scalability, transparency, and inclusivity to maximize LLMs' impact on education (goyal et al., 2025; Balaji, 2025).

Findings

  1. Large language models (LLMs) are being integrated into foreign language education to address gaps in listening, speaking, reading, and writing through personalized, immersive experiences (Yuxuan et al., 2025). By leveraging natural language processing capabilities, LLMs enable real-time feedback, interactive practice, and tailored content, which adapt to individual learner proficiency levels. This approach not only enhances engagement but also mitigates the limitations of traditional classroom settings, particularly in resource-constrained environments. For example, S1 highlights how LLMs can simulate conversational scenarios, providing learners with opportunities to practice without the need for human instructors, thereby expanding access to language learning tools (Yuxuan et al., 2025).
  2. In K-12 education, LLMs are being evaluated through specialized benchmarks like E-EVAL, which assesses their performance in subjects ranging from mathematics to ethics (Hou et al., 2024). This benchmarking framework identifies strengths and weaknesses of LLMs in culturally and linguistically specific contexts, such as Chinese education. Chinese-dominant models, for instance, outperform English-dominant counterparts in tasks requiring nuanced understanding of local curricula, suggesting that LLMs can be fine-tuned to align with regional pedagogical standards. Such evaluations are critical for ensuring LLMs meet the rigorous demands of formal education systems (Hou et al., 2024).
  3. Multimodal learning assistants, combining LLMs with computer vision, are transforming personalized education by addressing diverse learner needs through visual and textual analysis (Hosam, 2026). These systems integrate real-time data from both audiovisual inputs and textual responses, enabling adaptive planning and interactive feedback. For example, S6 describes a framework where computer vision tracks student behavior while LLMs generate context-aware explanations, creating a holistic learning experience. This integration allows for dynamic adjustments to teaching strategies, enhancing both engagement and comprehension in varied educational settings (Hosam, 2026).
  4. In rural K-12 contexts, LLMs offer potential to alleviate teacher workloads and provide 24/7 academic support, though adoption faces infrastructural and cultural barriers (goyal et al., 2025). Student volunteers in India report that LLMs can personalize learning and offer instant feedback, which is particularly valuable in areas with limited educational resources. However, challenges such as unreliable internet access, teacher training gaps, and parental skepticism hinder widespread implementation. These findings underscore the need for targeted interventions to bridge the digital divide and ensure equitable access to LLM-driven educational tools (goyal et al., 2025).
  5. LLMs are being applied to automate course evaluation in higher education, offering scalable solutions for analyzing teaching effectiveness and curricular design (Yuan and Hu, no date). By fine-tuning LLMs with domain-specific data, institutions can generate detailed feedback on course content, student engagement, and pedagogical strategies. S9 demonstrates that LLMs can process large volumes of student responses and instructor assessments, identifying patterns that inform curriculum improvements. While their accuracy depends on prompt engineering, this application highlights LLMs' potential to streamline administrative tasks and enhance data-driven decision-making in academia (Yuan and Hu, no date).

Limitations

  • The integration of LLMs in education faces ethical challenges, such as data privacy risks and algorithmic bias, which are highlighted in S8. Further evidence on mitigation strategies for these issues would strengthen the analysis. (Balaji, 2025)
  • Current benchmarks for evaluating LLMs in education, like E-EVAL in S2, focus on specific domains but lack comprehensive frameworks for diverse educational contexts. Expanding such benchmarks could address this gap. (Hou et al., 2024)
  • Infrastructure limitations and AI literacy gaps, as noted in S7, hinder rural adoption of LLMs. Evidence on scalable solutions for under-resourced settings would clarify practical barriers. (goyal et al., 2025)

The integration of LLMs and education is getting closer and closer, however, there is currently no benchmark for evaluating LLMs that focuses on the Chinese K-12 education domain. With E-EVAL, we aim to analyze the strengths and limitations of LLMs in educational applications, and to contribute to the progress and development of Chinese K-12 education and LLMs.
