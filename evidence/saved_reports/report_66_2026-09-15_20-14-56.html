Research Question

How does generative AI affect student learning outcomes in higher education?

Summary

Generative AI (GenAI) has shown mixed but notable impacts on student learning outcomes in higher education, with its effectiveness mediated by factors such as student engagement and AI literacy. Studies highlight its potential to enhance academic support through functions like instant feedback, research assistance, and improved writing, which can strengthen problem-solving skills and organizational abilities (Alkhatib et al., 2026). However, these benefits are contingent on students’ ability to engage critically with AI tools and develop literacy in their use, as demonstrated by the mediating role of engagement and AI literacy in shaping learning outcomes (Alkhatib et al., 2026). For instance, GenAI-based assessment systems offer adaptive, real-time feedback that may improve learning effectiveness by addressing individual needs, though their impact depends on institutional design and faculty integration (D, 2026). This aligns with broader frameworks advocating for pedagogical alignment and institutional readiness to maximise GenAI’s potential, as emphasised in studies exploring its integration into curricula (Riaz and Mushtaq, 2024).

Empirical evidence reveals both opportunities and limitations in GenAI’s application. While some studies report increased student engagement and academic performance, others caution against over-reliance on AI, noting variable effectiveness across disciplines and concerns about reduced critical thinking (Hon, 2025). For example, a survey of 399 Hong Kong students revealed positive perceptions of GenAI’s utility in personalised learning and research, but also highlighted ethical concerns and fears of diminished personal development (Chan and Hu, 2023). Similarly, Nigerian higher education institutions have explored GenAI’s role in personalised learning, though challenges such as academic integrity and the need for robust policy frameworks remain unresolved (Elenode, 2026). These findings underscore the importance of balancing AI’s capabilities with human oversight, as well as addressing disparities in access and training to ensure equitable outcomes.

The practical implications of GenAI integration depend on contextual factors, including institutional support, pedagogical strategies, and student preparedness. Qualitative analyses suggest that successful implementation requires aligning AI tools with educational goals, such as fostering creativity through AI art applications or enhancing interdisciplinary projects (Rong et al., 2025). However, the lack of standardised metrics and long-term data complicates assessments of GenAI’s impact, with some studies noting gaps in understanding its effects on deeper learning or ethical awareness (Shuvo et al., 2025). This highlights the need for further research to evaluate longitudinal outcomes and address concerns about AI’s role in perpetuating biases or reducing accountability in academic work (Elenode, 2026).

In conclusion, GenAI presents transformative possibilities for higher education, but its success hinges on thoughtful integration that prioritizes student agency, ethical considerations, and institutional adaptability. While it can enhance engagement, provide personalised support, and streamline academic processes, its limitations—such as over-reliance, disciplinary variability, and ethical risks—demand careful management. Future efforts should focus on developing scalable frameworks that balance technological innovation with pedagogical integrity, ensuring that GenAI serves as a tool to augment, rather than replace, human-driven learning outcomes.

Limitations

  • The variability in study methodologies (e.g., qualitative vs. quantitative) limits comparability of findings (Hon, 2025). Further evidence from mixed-methods studies could clarify the conditions under which GenAI most effectively enhances learning outcomes.
  • Contextual factors like institutional support and pedagogical alignment remain underexplored (Riaz and Mushtaq, 2024). More research on implementation strategies across diverse educational settings would strengthen generalisability.
  • Mixed evidence on long-term impacts highlights gaps in sustained outcome measurement (Hon, 2025). Longitudinal studies tracking student progress over time could address this limitation.
  • Variability in student engagement and AI literacy as mediating factors requires deeper exploration (Alkhatib et al., 2026). Future research should quantify these dynamics across demographic and disciplinary groups.
  • Ethical concerns and access disparities are underreported in existing studies (Chan and Hu, 2023). Systematic analysis of equity impacts and policy frameworks would improve understanding of GenAI’s broader educational implications.
